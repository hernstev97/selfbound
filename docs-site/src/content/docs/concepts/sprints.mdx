A sprint is a stretch of time, usually two weeks, with a goal and a set of issues you plan to finish. You plan
it, start it, work through it and complete it, deciding what happens to anything left over.

## One sprint for all teams

In Selfbound, sprints don't belong to a team. This is a deliberate choice. As one person you have one stream of
time, however many teams your work is spread across. Sprints per team split that time into parallel plans
that nobody keeps up, so Selfbound plans it once: a sprint holds issues from any team, and at most one sprint runs
at a time.

For the same reason nothing rolls over silently. When you complete a sprint, every unfinished issue needs a
decision, so the same issues don't drift from sprint to sprint unnoticed.

## The life of a sprint

A sprint is in one of three states:

1. **Planning**: you pick issues and set a goal and capacity. Nothing is running yet.
2. **Active**: you started it. Issues added from now on count as **added late**.
3. **Closed**: you completed it. Its contents are frozen as a record of what happened.

A sprint never starts or ends on its own when its dates arrive. Both are deliberate steps. You can plan the
next sprint while the current one is still running.

## Plan a sprint

<Steps>
1. Choose **Plan sprint** in the command menu (<Kbd keys="mod+k" />), or click **Plan sprint** in the sprint view
   (<Kbd keys="g s" />).
2. If no sprint is in planning yet, fill in **Create sprint**: **Name**, **Start**, **End** and an optional
   **Goal**. By default a sprint runs two weeks, starting the day after the current sprint ends, or next
   Monday if no sprint is open.
3. On the planning page, move issues from the **Backlog** column on the left into the sprint on the right.
4. Set a **Capacity** and check the planned points against it.
5. Click **Start sprint** when you're ready.
</Steps>

If you start a sprint before its planned start date, it starts today and keeps its length, so its end moves
forward too. If you complete a sprint before its planned end, its end date becomes today.

<Screenshot name="sprint-plan" alt="Sprint planning: the backlog of all teams on the left, the planned sprint with points and capacity on the right" />

The **Backlog** column shows open, accepted issues without a sprint from all active teams. Triage issues,
dormant teams and knowledge-only teams are left out. Filters and display options work as in any list.

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| <Kbd keys="arrowright" /> | Move the focused or selected issues into the sprint |
| <Kbd keys="arrowleft" /> | Send them back to the backlog |
| <Kbd keys="tab" /> | Switch between the two columns |
| <Kbd keys="x" /> | Select |
| <Kbd keys={['alt+arrowup', 'alt+arrowdown']} /> | Change the order |

You can also drag issues between the columns.

### Capacity and the priority budget

Capacity is the number of points you expect to finish. Selfbound suggests one: the average of points completed in
your last three sprints. Click **Apply** to take it, or type your own. The sprint column shows planned points
against capacity, warns when you plan more than fits, and counts issues **without estimate**, because those
add nothing to the total.

Above the backlog, a strip shows each team's open Urgent and High issues against the
[priority budget](/concepts/issues/#priority) (1 Urgent, 3 High).

## During the sprint

Press <Kbd keys="g s" /> (**Go to: Sprint**) to open the running sprint. The header shows the goal, **Progress**
in points, how many **Issues** are done, the **Capacity** with the share planned, how many issues were
**Added late**, and the **Date range** with the days remaining. By default the list is grouped into
**Planned** and **Added later**, then by status.

To put issues into a sprint from anywhere:

- <Kbd keys="shift+c" /> (**Sprint …**) opens the sprint picker: the running sprint, sprints in planning, or
  **No sprint**.
- **Add to the sprint** in the command menu adds the selection to the running sprint, or, if none runs, to the
  sprint in planning.
- A new issue created in the sprint view goes into the sprint.

## Complete a sprint

Choose **Complete sprint** in the command menu, or **Close** in the sprint view. The completion page lists every
issue that is not done yet.

- **Done** issues count as completed automatically. **Canceled** and duplicate issues are recorded as canceled.
- Every other issue needs a decision:

| Key | Decision | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| <Kbd keys="1" /> | **Next sprint** | Carried over into the sprint chosen under **Carry over to**: a sprint in planning or a **New sprint (will be created)** |
| <Kbd keys="2" /> | **Backlog** | Leaves the sprint; its status stays |
| <Kbd keys="3" /> | **Cancel** | Leaves the sprint and gets the status Canceled |
| <Kbd keys={['0', 'backspace']} /> | **Clear decision** | |

Select several issues to decide for all of them at once. Add a short retrospective if you like, then click
**Close** with the sprint's name, or press <Kbd keys="mod+enter" />, once every issue has a decision. Nothing is
moved without your decision.

## Past sprints

**All sprints** in the sprint view (or **Go to: All sprints** in the command menu) lists every sprint with its goal, date range, points done against
planned, how much was added late and how many issues were carried over. Cycles imported from Linear appear
here as well.

A closed sprint keeps its record: which issues were planned or added late, what happened to each, and the
estimate each completed issue had at that moment. Reopening an issue or changing its estimate later does not
change that record or the capacity suggestion.

<Aside type="tip">
Sprints work without estimates too. Progress and capacity then have nothing to count, but planned versus
added late, carry-over and the record of each sprint still work.
</Aside>
